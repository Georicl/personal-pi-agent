# Evidence Policy

## Source levels

- **Primary:** original research, official documentation, standards, source code, first-party datasets, or direct records.
- **Secondary:** systematic reviews, high-quality surveys, technical analyses, or documentation that synthesizes primary sources.
- **Tertiary:** summaries, news, search snippets, community posts, and unsourced explanations. Use for discovery, not as sole support for important claims.

## Claim handling

For each material claim, record:

- the source that supports it;
- whether support is direct or indirect;
- the relevant date and version;
- important population, dataset, or environment limits;
- confidence and unresolved contradictions.

## Comparison rules

- Compare methods on matched inputs, preprocessing, evaluation units, and metrics whenever possible.
- Do not equate association with prediction, early detection, mechanism, or causality.
- Do not treat repeated observations from one subject or project as independent validation units.
- Do not hide null, negative, or contradictory findings.

## Citation rules

- Cite the closest source to the supported claim.
- Prefer stable identifiers or direct authoritative links.
- Avoid quoting more text than needed.
- Clearly label synthesis and inference that are not stated by the source.
